## Timers and a stepping wall clock

Done. A countdown used to be stored as `fire_at = now + secs`, a wall-clock instant, so a step of the system clock while it ran made it ring early or late, or be announced as missed (a clock 3 h behind at boot, a 10 minute timer set 20 s in, network time arriving 20 s later: announced as missed 10,220 s late). The same flaw sat in every other duration the core measured: the 120 s confirmation window of a held action (REQ-SEC-011, a security property: a backward step kept a held action releasable), the clarify window, conversation and speaker idle time, administrator sessions, the reset window and its cooldown, and the rate-limit buckets.

**What is true now.** `Clock` has a required `monotonic_secs()` next to `now()`. Everything that is a duration uses the monotonic reading; everything that is a point on the calendar keeps the wall clock. The table of which is which, the saved form of a countdown and how a restart reconciles it are in [architecture/agent-loop.md, "Time"](architecture/agent-loop.md). The spec rows REQ-TMR-002, 003, 006, 009, REQ-SEC-004, REQ-SEC-011, REQ-AST-007, REQ-AST-021, REQ-CFG-019 and REQ-CFG-069 say it.

**Decisions made on the way**

- A countdown timer no longer needs a valid wall clock (REQ-TMR-009 used to forbid setting, listing and ringing timers before it): it depends on nothing but the monotonic clock, and on a device with no network "ten minutes for the pasta" is the most useful thing to still work. Alarms and reminders at a time of day still wait for the calendar. REQ-DEV-018 ("MUST NOT fire a timer locally on an untrusted clock (REQ-TMR-009)") is about the device daemon's own timers and was left as it is; the maintainer may want to read it as covering alarms only.
- "Remind me in five minutes" and a snooze are durations and run on the monotonic clock. "Remind me at 5 pm" is a calendar point.
- The inbox (announcement `ttl_secs`, snooze, reply expiry, pruning) stays on the wall clock. Those records live in files and are compared across restarts, and their worst failure is a notification dropped or spoken late. Moving them needs the save-and-reconcile scheme timers now have, applied to the inbox and reply files. Not done.
- The stopwatch stays wall-clock by design (REQ-TMR-011, REQ-TMR-012).
- `Instant` does not advance while a machine is suspended. These devices do not suspend; `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` would fix a laptop but needs a `libc` call or a new dependency.

**Still open.** The time the program was off is counted with the wall clock at load, so a stale clock at that moment under-counts it (never a negative amount). Closing that is REQ-BRD-010 (hold until the clock is `synced`), separate work. The portal's reminders page says "nothing will ring until [the clock is set]" and does not say a timer will; the page is not part of this change.
